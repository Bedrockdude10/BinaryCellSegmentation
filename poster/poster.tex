  \begin{block}{Motivation}
    Pathology foundation models (UNI, 307M parameters; Virchow2, 632M) ease the
    labelled-data shortage, but they are hard to fine-tune, questioned under cross-site
    shift, and transfer poorly beyond H\&E. Edge microscopy, veterinary and rare-disease
    labs often have \textbf{only tens of annotated slides and a single GPU}.

    \medskip
    \heading{Question}
    Among lightweight encoders that can be trained from scratch, which is best in the
    low-data regime?
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Controlled design: only the encoder changes}
    \begin{center}

        box/.style={draw=vggC, line width=2pt, rounded corners=6pt, align=center,

      \node[box, minimum width=8cm] (in) {H\&E patch\\$256\times256$};
      \node[box, draw=swinC, fill=swinC!10, minimum width=14cm, right=2.5cm of in] (enc)
        {\textbf{Encoder (varied)}\\VGG $\cdot$ ResNet $\cdot$ Swin-T};
      \node[box, minimum width=12cm, below=2.8cm of enc] (dec)
        {Fixed U-Net decoder\\4 up-stages + skips};
      \node[box, minimum width=8cm, left=2.5cm of dec] (out) {Binary\\nucleus mask};
      \draw[arr] (in) -- (enc);
      \draw[arr] (enc) -- node[right, xshift=5mm]{4 skips} (dec);
      \draw[arr] (dec) -- (out);
    \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{center}
    The decoder, data pipeline, augmentation, optimiser and evaluation are shared, and
    every encoder returns four feature maps through one interface. Results are means over
    \textbf{5 training seeds} on one V100, with paired 95\% CIs.

    \medskip
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Encoder} & \textbf{First skip} & \textbf{Params} \\
      \midrule
      VGG (plain $3\times3$ stacks) & $256^2$ & 34.0M \\
      ResNet (residual blocks)      & $256^2$ & 37.5M \\
      Swin-T (scratch / ImageNet)   & $64^2 \to$ upsampled & 86.8M \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Datasets}
    \heading{PanNuke}
    19 tissue types, $256\times256$ patches. We train on fold1 (2,656 patches),
    validate on fold2 and test on fold3.

    \heading{MoNuSeg}
    \textbf{37} training slides ($1000\times1000$, 7 organs) and 14 test slides,
    including organs never seen in training. Slides are tiled into $256\times256$
    patches and split by slide.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Why should CNNs win here?}
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{Skip resolution.} Nucleus boundaries are a few pixels wide. VGG keeps
        full resolution at its first skip; Swin's $4\times4$ tokens downsample $4\times$
        immediately, and upsampling can't recover the detail.
      \item \textbf{Inductive bias vs data.} Attention has to learn the locality that
        convolution builds in, and a few thousand patches isn't enough data for that,
        even with ImageNet weights.
      \item \textbf{Input scale.} On $256^2$ patches, VGG's receptive field already
        covers most of the input by stage 4.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Limitations}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item 5 seeds on one fixed split, so split variance isn't bounded.
      \item No learning-rate sweep, and the LR schedule differs between CNN and Swin.
      \item Only three encoders. At 34M parameters VGG isn't a truly compact model.
      \item Binary masks only, not instance separation. H\&E stains only.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}
